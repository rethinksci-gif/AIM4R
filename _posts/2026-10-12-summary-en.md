<h3>Provenance-grouped splits expose leakage in perovskite ML</h3>
<p class="pick-why"><span class="label">Why read it</span>Open it to see how grouping perovskite ML splits by duplicate recipe, publication and institution changes which model ranks best, and why random-split results may not transfer.</p>
<p class="pick-actions"><a class="read-original" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12766v1">Read the original ↗</a><span class="pick-meta">arXiv · Uncertainty, shift &amp; provenance in materials/process data · Published 2026-10-08 · Collected 2026-10-12</span></p>
<div class="pick-body">
<h4 data-block="advance">What&#x27;s new</h4>
<p>The authors map institutional provenance in the Perovskite Database and replace the usual random split with a hierarchy of grouped splits that progressively isolate duplicate devices, publications and institutions. Benchmarking thirteen models across these splits is reported as new state-of-the-art performance, but no metric, error or external baseline is given in the abstract; the comparison point is random-split evaluation itself.</p>
<h4 data-block="evidence">Key evidence</h4>
<p>Abstract-level, preprint; validation setting and independent replication are not reported. For perovskite solar cells, nearly two-thirds of curated devices in the Perovskite Database (size not reported) share an exact recipe with another device, so random splits are reported to reward recall of historical data rather than physical generalisation. Across the thirteen benchmarked models, tree ensembles lead on random splits while their ranking collapses on genuinely unseen institutions and future discoveries, where composition-aware neural architectures lead; feature importance places device architecture and fabrication methodology on par with absorber chemistry.</p>
<h4 data-block="significance">Why it matters for R&amp;D</h4>
<p>For teams screening new absorber compositions, the practical question is whether a model that wins a random-split benchmark still ranks candidates from an unseen institution or architecture. Editor&#x27;s inference: the reported ranking reversal suggests model choice should be conditioned on intended use — interpolating among known recipes versus extrapolating to unseen institutions — though the abstract gives no metric or uncertainty to size that risk.</p>
<h4 data-block="boundaries">Limits</h4>
<p>Not shown: any quantitative metric, error or uncertainty for the thirteen models, the size of the Perovskite Database, experimental or field validation of predictions, and independent replication. Because only the abstract was read, checks that are absent from the text — such as cross-batch or cross-supplier tests — cannot be distinguished from checks that were never performed. Transfer to another database requires auditable provenance labels for devices, publications and institutions.</p>
<h4 data-block="next_step">Next step</h4>
<p>Read the abstract first (the only substantive text supplied), then ask the authors how the future-discoveries split is constructed and for per-model metrics and uncertainty at each split level. Editorial suggestion for a small check: hold out one institution&#x27;s devices as a group and compare the grouped-split ranking with the random-split ranking for the same models and metric.</p>
