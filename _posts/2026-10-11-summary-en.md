<h3>ML force-field errors traced to compression asymmetry</h3>
<p class="pick-why"><span class="label">Why read it</span>A 7,599-configuration DFT benchmark that shows which local environments make universal ML force fields fail, before you trust one for high-entropy or anion-mixed compositions.</p>
<p class="pick-actions"><a class="read-original" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09837v2">Read the original ↗</a><a class="read-fulltext" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09837">Full-text version read ↗</a><span class="pick-meta">arXiv · AI for materials &amp; polymers · Published 2026-10-07 · Collected 2026-10-11</span></p>
<div class="pick-body">
<h4 data-block="advance">What&#x27;s new</h4>
<p>Rather than reporting aggregate accuracy, the preprint splits force error by signed distance deviation from training-reference medians and by element, moving past coverage-distance explanations alone. It adds parameter-matched radial versus spherical-harmonic comparisons trained on identical splits and two random seeds, so the effect of angular representation on elemental fitting can be read off directly.</p>
<h4 data-block="evidence">Key evidence</h4>
<p>Full text, preprint, simulation only. Eleven pretrained models were evaluated against DFT energies, forces and stresses on 7,599 multicomponent configurations (1,867 metal or intermetallic, 3,326 oxygen-containing, 2,406 oxygen-free). After matching element pairs and absolute distance-offset bins, compression-side mean errors were 234–249 meV Å-1 versus 128–129 meV Å-1 on the extension side, ratios of 1. 81–1. 95. Parameter-matched spherical-harmonic models lowered test force error for 38 of 43 elements at second order (mean 19. 1%) and 40 of 43 at fourth order (mean 39. 8%) against a 212,288-parameter radial baseline.</p>
<h4 data-block="significance">Why it matters for R&amp;D</h4>
<p>For teams screening mixed-anion or high-entropy candidates, the directional asymmetry argues for ranking candidate force fields on compressed short contacts rather than average force RMSE. Editor&#x27;s inference: because the benchmark is computational and the element-level findings are correlational, using these signatures as a screening filter still requires checking against the specific chemistries and strain states of the intended workflow.</p>
<h4 data-block="boundaries">Limits</h4>
<p>No experimental validation and no independent replication are reported, and neither the data nor the analysis code is deposited publicly. The one-dimensional overlap metrics do not establish equivalence of full chemical environments or rank the reference datasets, and OMat24 already covers these environments broadly, which blurs the coverage story.</p>
<h4 data-block="next_step">Next step</h4>
<p>Start with Figure 9, which carries the signed-distance error curves and the dimer curvature comparison across all eleven models. Ask the authors for per-element error tables and whether the compression-extension ratio persists at finite temperature and under imposed strain.</p>
