<h3>Layered verification gate catches 81% of mined-data errors</h3>
<p class="pick-why"><span class="label">Why read it</span>If you build or consume literature-mined property datasets, this preprint reports measured catch and miss rates for a layered verification gate.</p>
<p class="pick-actions"><a class="read-original" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34642v1">Read the original ↗</a><span class="pick-meta">arXiv · Uncertainty, shift &amp; provenance in materials/process data · Published 2026-10-25 · Collected 2026-11-02</span></p>
<div class="pick-body">
<h4 data-block="advance">What&#x27;s new</h4>
<p>The change is a layered gate rather than a single-rule check: schema and provenance checks, physical admissibility, cross-source consensus, model auditing and suspicion-ranked review are combined. The reported single-rule comparator is standard symmetry rules alone, which caught 21 of the 30 defects found in accepted values.</p>
<h4 data-block="evidence">Key evidence</h4>
<p>Abstract only; preprint, not peer reviewed; validation is mixed (production run, curated audit, measured tensors, benchmark). The gate halted 402 of 816 candidate values in an electro-optic property database, yet an audit of 112 accepted values against curated references found 30 defective, 21 caught by symmetry rules alone; on measured BaTiO3 and KNbO3 tensors, constitutive identities caught 8 of 10 single-value errors. In a controlled benchmark the framework caught 81% of errors at 85% precision and brought held-out accuracy within 3% of a clean-data model; 89% of the twenty largest values in uncleaned data were errors.</p>
<h4 data-block="significance">Why it matters for R&amp;D</h4>
<p>Editor&#x27;s inference: teams that screen literature values before selecting a formulation or training a model should budget for residual errors even after gating; the reported concentration of errors among the largest values means top-ranked candidates can be extraction artifacts.</p>
<h4 data-block="boundaries">Limits</h4>
<p>Abstract only, so method details and error taxonomy are not assessable here; controlled-benchmark size and composition are not reported, independent replication is not reported, and cross-supplier validation is not addressed. Transfer beyond tensor-like electro-optic properties depends on having governing equations or symmetry rules for the target property.</p>
<h4 data-block="next_step">Next step</h4>
<p>Editor&#x27;s suggestion: test the gate on one held-out curated batch from your own extraction, comparing it with symmetry-only screening on defective-value recall and precision; also ask the authors for the benchmark composition and the error types among the accepted-but-defective values.</p>

<h3>MatOS: an agent OS for materials workflows</h3>
<p class="pick-why"><span class="label">Why read it</span>Open the original to check how MatOS organizes reusable materials-research assets and whether its benchmark gains hold once splits and baselines are inspected.</p>
<p class="pick-actions"><a class="read-original" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34203v1">Read the original ↗</a><span class="pick-meta">arXiv · Autonomous experiments &amp; experiment design · Published 2026-10-25 · Collected 2026-11-02</span></p>
<div class="pick-body">
<h4 data-block="advance">What&#x27;s new</h4>
<p>MatOS packages data, tools, knowledge, skills, roles and workflows as reusable assets, runs tasks as explicit resumable execution graphs with scientific checks, and revises assets from execution records and user feedback. The abstract reports an 11.15% improvement over the previously best agent on ResearchClawBench with the same base model, but does not define the score metric.</p>
<h4 data-block="evidence">Key evidence</h4>
<p>Abstract only; preprint. In eight research cases, the system narrowed more than 30 million candidate structures to 97 solid-electrolyte candidates, identified 89 halide variants with higher computed conductivity than their parent structures, and linked force errors of 11 pretrained interatomic potentials to local atomic environments. With a common base model, it exceeded Codex CLI by 2.13% on MatTools and 4.17% on SciAgentGym; the margin versus Claude Code is not reported. Two supported methods reduced errors by 3.1% and 13.6% relative to their baselines; validation setting, benchmark splits, held-out conditions, uncertainty estimates, independent replication and laboratory confirmation are not reported.</p>
<h4 data-block="significance">Why it matters for R&amp;D</h4>
<p>Editor&#x27;s inference: for a team deciding which formulation to test next, the reported more than 30 million-to-97 shortlist is the operationally relevant workflow, but it is a computational candidate list, not a synthesis decision. Reusable assets would matter most across repeated screening campaigns; cross-project transfer is not reported.</p>
<h4 data-block="boundaries">Limits</h4>
<p>The abstract does not report benchmark splits, base-model identity, held-out conditions, independent replication, laboratory confirmation or uncertainty estimates; this absence does not establish that such tests were not performed. Transfer to other chemistries, instruments or laboratories remains unverified, and the numerical margin versus Claude Code is missing.</p>
<h4 data-block="next_step">Next step</h4>
<p>Editor&#x27;s suggestion: the abstract is the only visible section, so request the benchmark splits, base-model identity and any held-out evaluation from the authors; a small check would compare MatOS and Codex CLI on a held-out task subset using the same base model and the reported benchmark score.</p>
