<h3>TexFlow models texture as SO(3) distributions</h3>
<p class="pick-why"><span class="label">Why read it</span>Read the original if you need a process-conditioned probabilistic texture model whose samples can be checked directly against EBSD or CPFEM pole figures and ODF sections.</p>
<p class="pick-actions"><a class="read-original" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28363v1">Read the original ↗</a><a class="read-fulltext" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28363">Full-text version read ↗</a><span class="pick-meta">arXiv · AI for materials &amp; polymers · Published 2026-10-20 · Collected 2026-10-26</span></p>
<div class="pick-body">
<h4 data-block="advance">What&#x27;s new</h4>
<p>TexFlow replaces classical ODF and pole-figure descriptions, and prior ML texture models built on spherical-harmonic coefficients, Euler-angle generators or inverse pole figure images, with a conditional normalizing flow that learns p(R|c) over rotations. Rotations are encoded in a six-dimensional Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization representation and mapped back to valid SO(3) rotations by construction, with temperature or deformation state entering as conditioning variables. Hopf-fibration plots are added as a compact global visualization that retains full rotation information, resolving the pole-figure ambiguity the paper illustrates. No alternative model baseline is reported in the supplied text.</p>
<h4 data-block="evidence">Key evidence</h4>
<p>Validation is mixed: unconditional EBSD on AM SS316L (2,305 rotations), forsterite (1,032) and glaucophane (8,840); conditional EBSD on hot-rolled Ti-6Al-4V with temperature at 9 values, reduction at 4 values, 2 directions (RD/TD) and 56 conditions, not all available; and conditional CPFEM on copper at 50 timesteps. Comparison is against ground-truth distributions using pole figures, ODF sections, Hopf plots, and the metrics MM-KLD, MM-EMD and texture index. The reported results in the supplied text are qualitative and only for AM SS316L: TF samples reproduce {100} and {110} clusters aligned with build direction with agreeing peak MRD strengths and broadened rather than artificially sharp components. Numerical metric values and train/test split details are not reported; this is a preprint, not peer reviewed.</p>
<h4 data-block="significance">Why it matters for R&amp;D</h4>
<p>The practical target is querying texture at process conditions not measured, which matters when selecting the next rolling temperature or reduction to test instead of running another CPFEM case. Editor&#x27;s inference: a conditional model of this kind could screen condition combinations before committing EBSD time, but only if its predictions at held-out conditions are shown to hold; the supplied text reports qualitative agreement, not that test.</p>
<h4 data-block="boundaries">Limits</h4>
<p>Not reported in the available text: MM-KLD, MM-EMD or texture-index values, train/test split protocol, cross-batch or cross-supplier tests, and uncertainty estimates. Results for forsterite, glaucophane, Ti-6Al-4V and copper are limited to dataset descriptions in the supplied text, so multimodal and conditional claims rest mainly on the SS316L case. The comparison is against ground-truth data, not against Taylor, VPSC or CPFEM predictions head to head, and no independent replication is reported.</p>
<h4 data-block="next_step">Next step</h4>
<p>Start with Table 1 in Section 5 for materials, symmetries, conditions, data sizes and sources, then Section 5.1 for the first ground-truth comparison. Ask the authors for per-case MM-KLD, MM-EMD and texture-index values and for the held-out condition protocol. Editor&#x27;s suggestion: hold out one temperature/reduction condition, train on the rest, and compare TF samples against that condition&#x27;s EBSD via pole figures and texture index.</p>

<h2 id="topic-data-trust">Data Context, Validation &amp; Trust</h2>

<article class="pick" id="pick-2" data-track="main">
<p class="pick-badges"><span class="badge" data-kind="kind" data-value="preprint">Preprint</span><span class="badge" data-kind="access" data-value="abstract_or_summary">Abstract only</span><span class="badge" data-kind="review" data-value="preprint">Not peer reviewed</span></p>
<h3>Value-level provenance in an ELN shared by humans and LLM agents</h3>
<p class="pick-why"><span class="label">Why read it</span>For teams that keep derived numbers in an ELN: the abstract lays out a concrete schema binding each stored value to its source file and extractor version, plus an automated drift check.</p>
<p class="pick-actions"><a class="read-original" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27859v1">Read the original ↗</a><span class="pick-meta">arXiv · Uncertainty, shift &amp; provenance in materials/process data · Published 2026-10-20 · Collected 2026-10-26</span></p>
<div class="pick-body">
<h4 data-block="advance">What&#x27;s new</h4>
<p>The initial ELN preserved the sample-to-raw-file link but lost the raw-file-to-stored-value link, mainly because manual entries carried no file reference or extractor version. The rework makes every stored value record its source file, DFR plugin and version, any interactive analysis selection, or the server-side formula behind a derived quantity, and a drift check re-runs each DFR on the stored files. The comparators are that initial structure and five other platforms, of which the abstract says none binds an individual stored number to both its source file and its extractor version.</p>
<h4 data-block="evidence">Key evidence</h4>
<p>Abstract only; preprint, laboratory setting, no independent replication reported. A production audit in a materials laboratory, covering projects performed over almost 5 years, traced eight baseline studies from sample to stored value. Two end-to-end studies recorded by agents, in X-ray reflectivity and tribology, retained verifiable provenance for all stored values with zero drift. Five other platforms were compared. Materials, batches and measurement conditions are not reported in the abstract.</p>
<h4 data-block="significance">Why it matters for R&amp;D</h4>
<p>Editor&#x27;s inference: the failure mode named here, a stored number whose extractor version is unknown, is what makes re-analysis or audit of an older project hard, and it gets sharper when an LLM agent writes the entry. Transfer to another laboratory is conditional on that laboratory instrumenting its extraction step the same way; nothing here shows the schema fits non-X-ray or non-tribology pipelines.</p>
<h4 data-block="boundaries">Limits</h4>
<p>Zero drift covers two studies only; drift outside them is not reported, and no quantitative measure of provenance completeness is given beyond that. Single laboratory, no multi-site or cross-supplier test, no independent replication. Because only the abstract was read, the audit depth and the drift-check implementation cannot be verified, and a test absent from the abstract cannot be assumed absent from the paper.</p>
<h4 data-block="next_step">Next step</h4>
<p>Read the provenance-schema and drift-check sections to see what the check actually compares: the DFR version alone, or also the stored file. Editorial suggestion for a small check: re-extract one archived dataset with the current DFR, compare against the stored values on a held-out batch, and log every mismatch.</p>
