### [Bryan Cantrill on the ethics of undisclosed LLM writing](https://bcantrill.dtrace.org/2025/12/05/your-intellectual-fly-is-open/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Bryan Cantrill argues that using LLMs to write without disclosure is an intellectual faux pas, comparing it to walking around with one&\#x27;s fly open. He emphasizes that LLMs are lousy writers and, most importantly, that they are not the author, making undisclosed AI-generated work misleading and damaging to authenticity. The essay prompted substantial debate in the technology community about authorship, intellectual honesty, and writing as a form of thinking. The discussion highlights both support for disclosure as a matter of integrity and questions about whether objections to undisclosed LLM writing would weaken as the technology improves.

hackernews · cyb0rg0 · Sep 6, 11:56 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49585644)

**「Background」** Bryan Cantrill is a widely followed technology leader and essayist; his post uses the metaphor of a forgotten open fly to argue that using an LLM to write without disclosure is embarrassing and ethically problematic. His core premise is that even if the text seems plausible, LLM-generated writing carries identifiable stylistic markers, and that an unacknowledged AI author is not really the bylined person. The piece appeared on his long-running blog The Observation Deck in December 2025, and the ensuing Hacker News discussion centered on whether objections to undisclosed LLM writing are about current quality or about authenticity and authorship more broadly.

**「Community discussion」** Several commenters strongly agreed with Cantrill, adding that writing is thinking and that the personal quirks of an author are valuable to readers, while one commenter questioned whether the objection is fundamentally about current LLM quality or about a deeper norm. Others noted from editorial experience that letting individual voices come through is essential, making undisclosed LLM use particularly problematic.

### [Asahi Linux Announces Official Apple M3 Support](https://asahilinux.org/2026/09/m2-episode-1/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Asahi Linux project has announced official support for Apple M3 chips, extending its reverse-engineered Linux support to Apple&\#x27;s latest custom silicon. This milestone means owners of M3-based Macs can install and run Linux through Asahi&\#x27;s supported tooling rather than relying on older or ad-hoc workarounds. The project has previously focused on M1 and M2 series hardware, and this announcement represents incremental progress in bringing mainline Linux to Apple Silicon. As with earlier releases, support is built on significant reverse engineering of Apple&\#x27;s hardware, and community members note that some features, including sleep and HDMI output, remain blockers for broader adoption. The announcement was distributed via Phoronix and discussed extensively on Hacker News.

hackernews · mdp2021 · Sep 6, 14:08 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49586698)

**「Background」** Asahi Linux is a community project that ports Linux to Apple&\#x27;s M-series Macs, starting with M1 and M2 models by reverse-engineering Apple&\#x27;s proprietary hardware. Apple&\#x27;s custom silicon uses non-standard boot, GPU, and display controllers, so bringing Linux to it requires significant reverse engineering rather than just updating kernel drivers. Official support for Apple M3 Macs is an incremental milestone in this effort, though it currently lacks full GPU performance and sleep support because of incomplete DCP display controller support.

**「Impact」** Apple M3 Mac users now have a supported path to run Linux, expanding Asahi Linux&\#x27;s reach beyond M1 and M2 systems. However, community reports indicate incomplete hardware support, such as sleep and HDMI, may still limit daily-driver use.

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters generally praised the work as impressive reverse engineering, with one comparing it to repairing a spaceship while it launches, but they also highlighted practical limitations such as poor llama.cpp performance compared with Apple&\#x27;s Metal backend, missing sleep and HDMI support, and questions about dual-booting on M2 machines.

### [Nitter and XCancel resume after legal advice](https://github.com/zedeus/nitter/commit/1428b4c2b4246f92a7e5b2673438e5fb39fcc4a3) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Nitter and XCancel have resumed service after obtaining legal advice, continuing their operation as alternative frontends for X/Twitter. The announcement came through a GitHub commit on the zedeus/nitter repository and links to a legal page on xcancel.com. Nitter and XCancel allow users to view X posts without ads or tracking, reducing reliance on the official platform. Community members welcomed the continuation, noting that much important information is posted exclusively on X, while acknowledging legal and financial pressure on these projects.

hackernews · zImPatrick · Sep 6, 17:49 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49588988)

**「Background」** Nitter is an open-source, privacy-focused alternative interface for viewing X/Twitter content without ads, tracking, or heavy JavaScript, while XCancel provides a similar frontend. In late February 2025, X Corp. sent a cease-and-desist accusing Nitter of scraping and demanded that instances and the repository be taken down, prompting Nitter and XCancel to announce shutdowns and consult legal counsel. The developer subsequently announced on 6 February 2025 that the projects would resume service after legal advice.

**「Impact」** Users of Nitter and XCancel can again access X/Twitter content through privacy-preserving mirrors, helping those who rely on X-only posts avoid official tracking and advertising.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters were largely glad the project continues, emphasizing the value of alternative frontends for accessing X-exclusive information. Some expressed worry about legal pressure, the difficulty of moving users to decentralized alternatives, and the broader challenge of breaking out of walled gardens.
