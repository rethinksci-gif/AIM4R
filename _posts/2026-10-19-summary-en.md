<h3>ActMOF: a benchmark for active-learning MOF synthesis</h3>
<p class="pick-why"><span class="label">Why read it</span>If you plan a sparse-data synthesis campaign, ActMOF scores a diffraction-derived crystallinity index over 95 measured microwave syntheses and ranks 39 active-learning policies under one protocol, so you can see which optimizer to start with.</p>
<p class="pick-actions"><a class="read-original" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19404v1">Read the original ↗</a><a class="read-fulltext" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19404">Full-text version read ↗</a><span class="pick-meta">arXiv · AI for materials &amp; polymers · Published 2026-10-14 · Collected 2026-10-19</span></p>
<div class="pick-body">
<h4 data-block="advance">What&#x27;s new</h4>
<p>ActMOF turns 95 measured microwave MOF-321 syntheses into an emulated landscape of 6,101,172 encoded conditions scored by a crystallinity index q, then compares 39 active-learning policies under one protocol instead of picking an optimizer by intuition. The selected policies were then run in the wet lab on six reticularly related aluminum MOFs, each with a fixed 12-experiment budget.</p>
<h4 data-block="evidence">Key evidence</h4>
<p>MOF-321 conditions span five variables (aluminum precursor amount, modulator amount, added solvent volume, microwave time, microwave temperature); q is a diagnostic PXRD peak intensity divided by its FWHM, and the landscape is sparse, with top 1% q at a threshold of 42,463 and top 0.1% above 100,288. All 39 policies started from three random experiments then batches of three to a 120-experiment budget (39 iterations); the GP family placed among the leaders, and tree ensembles gained no measurable advantage despite the emulator being a random forest. Wet-lab transfer to CAU-23, MIL-160, CAU-10, MIL-53-muc, MTV-MOF-303 and MTV-MIL-160 gave crystalline products within 12 experiments each; this is a preprint, not peer reviewed, with no independent replication reported.</p>
<h4 data-block="significance">Why it matters for R&amp;D</h4>
<p>The engineering question is budget allocation: how many experiments to spend on a new linker before giving up. Editor&#x27;s inference: a 12-experiment GP-guided campaign is a plausible starting plan for Al-carboxylates that keep the rod SBU and the same microwave protocol, and that transfer should be treated as conditional on both being held fixed.</p>
<h4 data-block="boundaries">Limits</h4>
<p>The landscape is an emulation and is explicitly not a prediction for any given unmeasured condition; the authors note tree-based policies may look artificially strong because the emulator is a random forest. q is only a first-stage crystallinity metric, with stability and sorption left as downstream checks, and no cross-supplier, batch-to-batch or scale-up data appear. Policy rankings have not been validated outside aluminum-carboxylate MOFs.</p>
<h4 data-block="next_step">Next step</h4>
<p>Read Results and Discussion, especially the Figure 2 landscape description and the 39-policy benchmarking setup, to see the protocol a transfer would have to match. Editorial suggestion: on one target MOF, run a 12-experiment GP campaign against a 12-experiment random baseline under the same microwave protocol and compare best q plus PXRD phase purity, not only whether a product is crystalline.</p>

<h3>DiffGA: evolutionary search inside a frozen diffusion sampler</h3>
<p class="pick-why"><span class="label">Why read it</span>Read this if you need to steer a frozen crystal generator toward a black-box property target without retraining, and want an explicit account of where the MLFF oracle can and cannot be trusted.</p>
<p class="pick-actions"><a class="read-original" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14999v1">Read the original ↗</a><a class="read-fulltext" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14999">Full-text version read ↗</a><span class="pick-meta">arXiv · AI for materials &amp; polymers · Published 2026-10-10 · Collected 2026-10-19</span></p>
<div class="pick-body">
<h4 data-block="advance">What&#x27;s new</h4>
<p>DiffGA applies selection and genetic variation to partially denoised MatterGen horizon states inside the sampling trajectory, instead of fine-tuning the model or using differentiable score guidance. A Gaussian process fitted on the MLFF&#x27;s own graph embeddings replaces the oracle only after it prospectively ranks an unseen generation. The stated comparators are diffusion-only (generation g=1), compute-matched diffusion (N = P·B·G independent samples ranked post hoc by the same scorer), a pure GA at t_h = 0.002, and fine-tuned baselines.</p>
<h4 data-block="evidence">Key evidence</h4>
<p>Three tasks with frozen MatterGen backbones: Ti-Al-N with target shear modulus 160 ± 20 GPa, Cs-Sn-I with target energy above hull 0.1 ± 0.05 eV/atom, and MxCy (M ∈ Ti, V, Cr, Mo, W) maximizing shear modulus; three seeds and three population multipliers, with batch size B and generation count G unreported. The claim is a higher oracle-assessed stable-unique-novel hit rate than all four baselines, but no numeric hit-rate values are given. DFT re-evaluation covers a representative sample of top hits, naming WC and CrC. Full text, preprint, simulation only.</p>
<h4 data-block="significance">Why it matters for R&amp;D</h4>
<p>The recorded justification is that campaign cost is dominated by oracle evaluations, which is the same constraint as re-scoring candidates across changing property workflows. Editor&#x27;s inference: the promotion gate — a surrogate is used only after it ranks a fresh generation — is the transferable design pattern for any proxy-driven screening loop; the reported gains themselves are for MLFF-oracle crystal tasks only.</p>
<h4 data-block="boundaries">Limits</h4>
<p>No numeric hit rates, oracle-cost savings or surrogate accuracy values are reported, so the size of the improvement cannot be checked. DFT validation is a representative sample, not exhaustive; genetic operators are deliberately simple; the surrogate requires a force field exposing a usable structural embedding. All results are computational — no synthesis or characterization — and no independent replication is reported.</p>
<h4 data-block="next_step">Next step</h4>
<p>Read the Results subsection on steering frozen generative models, then section 1.2 for the baseline definitions. Editor&#x27;s suggestion for a transfer check: before letting a surrogate take over on your own oracle, hold out one batch of candidates per supplier or instrument and compare surrogate ranking against re-evaluated oracle values.</p>
